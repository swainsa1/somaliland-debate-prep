The topic in 30 seconds

Somaliland is the northern strip of Somalia, along the Gulf of Aden. It has governed itself since 1991, with its own government, army, money, passports and elections. On paper, though, almost every country still treats it as part of Somalia.

Israel became the first country to recognize it, in December 2025. The United States has not. A State Department report to Congress in 2026 said the U.S. still considers Somaliland part of Somalia.

The debate comes down to two things the U.S. cares about: rewarding a peaceful democracy (Aff) versus keeping borders stable and partners happy (Neg).

Current status (as of October 2026)

QuestionAnswer
Has the U.S. recognized Somaliland?No. It follows a "One Somalia" policy, which the State Department reaffirmed in a 2026 report to Congress.
Has anyone recognized it?Israel, on December 26, 2025. No other country has followed.
Is the U.S. talking to Somaliland?Yes, unofficially. U.S. officials have visited, and Somaliland's president met Senator Ted Cruz in Washington in September 2026.
Who supports recognition in Congress?Mostly some Republicans, including Sen. Ted Cruz. A bill, H.R. 3992, calls for recognition.
Watch out for fakes In January 2026 a fake press release claiming the U.S. had recognized Somaliland spread online. The U.S. Embassy in Mogadishu called it false. Always check claims about recognition against a reliable source.
