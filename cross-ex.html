Step 7

Cross-Ex Practice

Questions the other side might ask you. Answer out loud first, then open the model answer.

Use your own wordsThe model answers show the idea. In a round you can't read from notes, so practice saying each answer your own way.
Rule #1 of cross-exNever accept their version of the facts. If a question ends with "right?", watch out. It's a trap to make you agree. Fix the false part first, then answer.

When you're Affirmative

"In 2023 Somaliland lost Las Anod. So it doesn't control its territory, right?"

"No. Somaliland still controls most of its land, including its capital and its main port. And recognized countries have border disputes too: India and Pakistan both claim Kashmir. That isn't a reason to deny recognition."

"The U.S. recognizes lots of countries that aren't democracies. Why does democracy matter?"

"Democracy isn't the only reason. It's one good reason among several. Somaliland also meets the Montevideo test and sits in a key location." (When they attack one reason, don't over-defend it. Point to your others.)

"The U.S. doesn't recognize Taiwan either. So why would siding with Taiwan earn Somaliland recognition?"

"The U.S. doesn't officially recognize Taiwan, but it still supports Taiwan strongly. Somaliland took a real risk to stand with a U.S. friend, and partners who take risks for us deserve support."

"Somaliland chose to join Somalia in 1960. Why should it get to leave?"

"The union was never properly ratified, and northerners voted against the 1961 constitution. And choosing to join doesn't mean accepting being bombed by your own government. Somalia broke the deal."

"Can't the U.S. just use Berbera without recognizing Somaliland?"

"For short visits, maybe. But a real long-term base deal needs an official agreement with a recognized government. Without one, any deal can be dropped at any time."

"Your Heritage evidence is from 2021. Isn't it outdated?"

"The key facts haven't changed. China's base is still in Djibouti, right next to ours. Old evidence is fine when the facts are still true."

"The African Union says recognizing Somaliland is a dangerous precedent. Aren't they the experts on Africa?"

"The AU's own fact-finding mission in 2005 called Somaliland's case 'historically unique and self-justified.' The AU just never acted on its own report."

When you're Negative

"Somaliland meets all four Montevideo criteria. Isn't it already a country?"

"Even if it acts like one, recognition is a choice the U.S. makes based on its interests. And Somaliland doesn't control all the land it claims, as Las Anod shows."

"Somalia needs U.S. help against al-Shabaab. It would never cut ties, right?"

"Not necessarily. Somalia doesn't have to fully cut ties to hurt us. It could limit where U.S. forces operate, or turn to Turkey, Egypt or China. Why take the chance?"

"How would recognizing Somaliland hurt Americans?"

"If Somalia stops working with the U.S. against al-Shabaab, the group could grow stronger in East Africa and put Americans there at risk." (Don't say "terrorism in the U.S." You can't prove it.)

"Eritrea and South Sudan broke away and Africa was fine. Why would Somaliland be different?"

"Both held independence referendums that the country they left agreed to: Ethiopia accepted Eritrea's vote in 1993, and Sudan accepted South Sudan's in 2011 under a 2005 peace deal. Somalia has never agreed to let Somaliland go. Recognizing it anyway would mean taking sides in an unresolved dispute."

"The U.S. recognized Kosovo even though Serbia objected. Why not Somaliland?"

"Kosovo was backed by most of Europe and NATO. With Somaliland, almost no one is on board. Even after Israel recognized it, not a single other country followed. The U.S. would be nearly alone."

"Your CRS evidence never says recognition would end the partnership. So where's the risk?"

"It shows how much the fight depends on Somalia's cooperation: over 130 coordinated strikes in one year. And the White House itself warned in 2024 that a recognition deal 'threatens to disrupt the fight' against al-Shabaab."
